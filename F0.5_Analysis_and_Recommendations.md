## 0. Addendum: judge recalibration extension (14 September 2026, same day)

**What happened.** §6's recommendation was executed immediately: bar v2 was frozen (κ ≥ 0.6, ranking preserved, |contrast error| ≤ 0.02 on all four contrasts), five new candidates were added, and the adoption decision was re-run on the existing calibration data plus the new scoring. On the original 160 instances no candidate passed — Maverick closest at 0.0273. You then had it re-scored on **all 1,450 F0 instances** carrying an Opus verdict, where it passes: max contrast error 0.0170, κ 0.766, $0.0007/call. **Standing judge is now Llama 4 Maverick**, at ~$1 per F0.5-sized phase against ~$23 for Opus. Total cost $1.97, zero new Opus calls. Everything in §6 below is superseded by this; the rest of the document stands.

**Three things came out of the extension that are more important than the judge decision itself.**

**(a) The 160-instance calibration sample was badly unrepresentative, and had the wrong sign on the headline contrast.** Opus's own reference contrasts, subsample vs. full population:

| Opus contrast (dag − X) | n=160 | n=1450 | gap | sign flip |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| dag − full_history | **−0.0845** | **+0.0263** | −0.111 | **yes** |
| dag − oracle_tree | +0.0423 | +0.0740 | −0.032 | no |
| dag − sliding@1024 | +0.1528 | +0.2047 | −0.052 | no |
| dag − semantic@1024 | **−0.0897** | **+0.0234** | −0.113 | **yes** |

The 1,450 figures reconcile exactly with F0: dag − full pools to +0.0265 from F0's +2.7/+2.6 pp, and dag − tree to +0.0732 from F0's join-family +0.315/+0.328 across 33 of 145 scenarios. So the enlarged sample is the real population and **the 160-instance subsample is the outlier — by 11 points, with the sign reversed, on the study's headline comparison.** The likely mechanism is that the subsample was drawn family-proportionally *per instance* rather than as whole scenarios across all five methods, which unpairs every cross-method contrast. **Retire that sample. Any future calibration should sample whole scenarios across all methods**, which both fixes this and removes the resolution problem that forced the enlargement.

This does not invalidate the judge decision, because contrast *errors* are paired (candidate vs. Opus on the same instances) and are therefore robust to an unrepresentative draw — which is why §6's analysis, computed on the same bad subsample, still held up when checked against 1,450 (Maverick's dag − full error moved from −0.0075 to −0.0049). It does mean nothing else should ever be read off that subsample's levels.

**(b) F0 now has a full dual-judge replication sitting unreported in the results directory.** The enlarged run scored every F0 instance on all five calibration methods with Maverick. That is a complete second-judge pass over F0's headline and check-4 results:

| contrast | Opus 4.6 | Llama 4 Maverick |
|---|---|---|
| dag − full_history | +0.0263 | +0.0214 |
| dag − oracle_tree | +0.0740 | +0.0769 |
| dag − sliding@1024 | +0.2047 | +0.1877 |
| dag − semantic@1024 | +0.0234 | +0.0138 |

A cross-vendor judge with no family overlap with either responder reproduces every F0 conclusion. **This retires the self-preference concern that has been carried as an open risk since F0, and it should be a table in the paper.** It cost nothing extra and nobody has written it down.

**(c) The check-3 outlook is slightly better than F0 suggested.** `dag − semantic@1024` is +0.023 (Opus) / +0.014 (Maverick) on the full population — a tie or a shade ahead, not the loss the 160-instance subsample implied (−0.090). Still almost certainly inside noise, and it does not change the strategic read that the contribution narrows toward efficiency, leakage and joins. But run the paired bootstrap on this properly before writing check 3's framing, rather than inheriting "semantic retrieval ties oracle DAG" as settled.

**One honest qualification on the pass.** With SE ≈ 0.012 on each contrast error, every 95% CI extends past the ±0.02 bound (e.g. dag − sliding: −0.017 [−0.044, +0.010]). None excludes zero. So "passes bar v2" correctly means *no detectable method-differential bias, with point estimates inside tolerance* — not *proven within 0.02*. That is the right standard and it is what the bar was designed to test; it should just be written that way. Relatedly, Maverick was selected as closest on the 160 and confirmed on the 1,450 that contains it — a two-stage selection, but with 89% fresh data and a bar that the point estimates clear comfortably, this is not a concern worth acting on.

**Downgrade the Opus test–retest floor from "required" to "optional."** §6 asked for it to justify relaxing an item-level bar. With a paired contrast bar passing on 1,450 instances, it is no longer load-bearing — keep it as a ~$3 nicety if a reviewer asks how well the reference judge agrees with itself.

## 1. Bottom line

**The PASS is procedurally clean and I would not overturn it. But the two numbers the report leads with are the two least informative numbers in it, and the two most informative findings are buried — one of them behind a caveat that is factually wrong.**

Specifically:

- **Recall@15 = 0.993 is close to vacuous on this benchmark.** At k=15 the candidate pool holds 89.3% of the entire conversation history on average. A "candidate generator" that keeps nine turns in ten is not a filter, so a recall gate applied to it cannot fail. The executing session says as much; I want to put it more strongly, because the gate is check 2 of the pre-registered procedure and "check 2 passed" is going to get cited downstream as if it meant something it does not yet mean.
- **The k=15 quality arm is a replication of F0, not a new measurement** — the report says this too — and, separately, **the k=5 and k=10 arms are not independent measurements at all.** I reconstructed the call accounting (§2) and they are re-poolings of the k=15 answers with F0's full-history answers, not fresh generations. That is a defensible and economical design, but it changes how the k=5 row may be read, and the report does not state it.
- **The strongest result in F0.5 is the one the report warns you away from.** On the 49 scenarios where a five-turn pool actually suffices, restricted context scores +4.1 pp (Sonnet) and +9.5 pp (Haiku) over full history while using 80% fewer tokens. The report labels that subset "optimistic… dominated by short scenarios." It is not: those scenarios have *longer* full histories than the fail-open ones (1,698 vs 1,411 mean tokens). They are small-closure scenarios, not short ones — which is precisely the deployment case the project is built for.
- **The judge-calibration conclusion is wrong, and it cost $12.** All four candidates were rejected on a bar that measures a quantity no claim in this study depends on. Re-scored on the contrasts that the study actually reports, Llama 4 Maverick reproduces every decision-relevant comparison to within 2.7 checklist points and the headline one to within 0.8 pp, at 1/18th the cost. §6.

None of this changes the verdict. F0.5 says what it was meant to say: a cheap non-LLM candidate generator is not the bottleneck. It says it on a benchmark too short to make the question hard, which is the executing session's own top recommendation and now mine as well.

## 2. Verification: what I re-derived, and what reconciles

Following the F0 review's practice of checking arithmetic rather than accepting totals:

**Call accounting reconciles, and reveals the k-arm structure.** Dividing each response model's answer spend by its own reported per-answer cost gives ~146 Sonnet answers, ~141 Haiku answers, ~493 MiniMax answers — i.e. **one** arm of 145 for each Anthropic model and **three** for MiniMax. So `candidate_oracle@5` and `candidate_oracle@10` for Sonnet and Haiku were assembled, not generated: the k=15 answer is reused where the pool holds, F0's `full_history` answer where it fails open. The fingerprint is visible in the tables — `candidate_oracle@10` and `candidate_oracle@15` are identical in every by-family cell except one (Haiku, `long_noisy_side_thread`, 0.858 vs 0.883). Totals: 725 answers + 640 calibration + 731 judge routes = 2,096 against 2,124 reported, the 28-call gap consistent with smoke and retries. **This is fine as a design choice and saves real money; it needs to be stated in the report, because three k rows presented side by side read as three measurements.**

**The k=5 decomposition checks out against the report's own bootstrap.** Reconstructing the k=5 pooled score as a weighted average of the holds and fail-open subsets yields +4.1 pp (Sonnet) and +9.5 pp (Haiku) on the holds subset — matching the report's `candidate_oracle@5[recall-holds] vs full_history` bootstrap rows (+0.0391 and +0.0952) to the third decimal. The reconstruction is sound, and so is the report's own number; it is the *interpretation* attached to it that is wrong (§5).

**Recall arithmetic is internally consistent.** 144/145 = 0.993 uncapped; 136/137 excluding `new_root` = 0.993; `three_way_join` 7/8 = 0.875; semantic source 487/491 = 0.992. The single outright miss (`three_way_join_004`, t6–t7 of a nine-turn closure) is the only scenario failing strict recall at every k ≥ 15. No discrepancies.

**Retention arithmetic is consistent** (3.046/2.644 = 115.2%, etc.) — and meaningless, as the report itself flags, since the denominator's CI spans zero. Correctly reported, correctly not gated. It should not appear in a paper in this form.

## 4. What the k=15 PASS does and does not establish

The pre-registration was honoured: k=15 and the 0.85 threshold were frozen at commit 2a77d9b before any billed call, and were not moved afterwards. That is the part that matters for integrity and it is intact.

The problem is construct validity, not protocol. On benchmark 1.1:

| k | mean pool as fraction of full history | strict Recall |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 0.389 | 0.117 |
| 5 | 0.649 | 0.338 |
| 10 | 0.857 | 0.972 |
| 15 | 0.893 | 0.993 |

**There is no k at which the pool is simultaneously a meaningful filter and high-recall.** At k ≥ 10 the pool is most of the conversation; at k ≤ 5 recall collapses. That is not a property of the heuristics — it is a property of a benchmark whose histories average ~9 turns. The gate was sized for a problem the benchmark does not contain.

Two consequences:

1. **"Check 2 passed" should be written down with its qualifier attached**, everywhere it is carried forward, in the form: *a five-source non-LLM generator recovers the full gold closure inside a 15-turn pool on 99.3% of scenarios whose histories average nine turns.* Without the clause it will be read as a general claim about candidate generation and it is not one.
2. **Benchmark 1.2 (30–60 turn histories in the join and resume families) is now the highest-leverage single item on the project**, ahead of check 3 — because check 3 run on benchmark 1.1 will inherit exactly the same problem. At ~1,500 mean full-history tokens, every method in this study fits comfortably in any modern context window, so the efficiency claim is currently being measured in a regime where nobody would feel it. The executing session reaches the same conclusion in its recommendations; I would elevate it from "would be nice" to "blocking for the paper's efficiency claim."

## 5. The report under-sells its own best result

The report's framing: k=15 is a near-replication, "the informative sensitivity arm is k=5," and the `[recall-holds]` rows are "optimistic, because at small k that subset is dominated by short scenarios."

That last clause is wrong, and it is checkable from the report's own numbers. Decomposing the mean context-token columns:

- fail-open scenarios at k=5 (n=96): mean full history **1,411 tokens**, saving **0%**
- pool-holds scenarios at k=5 (n=49): mean full history **1,698 tokens**, restricted context 340 tokens, saving **80%**

The holds subset has *longer* histories. It is selected on having a **small gold closure**, not a short conversation — long history, few relevant turns. That is the canonical case the whole project exists to serve, and on it the result is:

| | restricted | full history | Δ | tokens |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sonnet 4.6 (n=49) | 0.937 | 0.896 | **+4.1 pp** | −80% |
| Haiku 4.5 (n=49) | 0.888 | 0.793 | **+9.5 pp** [+0.9, +18.2] | −80% |

Haiku's CI excludes zero. This is the only quality comparison anywhere in F0 or F0.5 whose confidence interval clears zero on a pre-registered-style contrast, and it is the same removal effect F0 found on `new_root`: the weaker, cheaper model gains most from *not being shown* irrelevant branches.

There is a second, equally useful fact in the same decomposition: **the savings concentrate exactly where candidate generation succeeds.** Big-closure scenarios (where the pool fails) are also the scenarios where the DAG saves least — a nine-turn closure over a twelve-turn history is barely a reduction. So the cases the candidate generator gets wrong are the cases where getting them right would have paid least. That is a genuinely favourable structural property for a deployed system and it is not stated anywhere in the report.

**Recommendation: restructure the k=5 presentation around the conditional result** — "on the 34% of scenarios where a five-turn pool suffices, +4/+10 pp at −80% tokens; on the rest, the system fails open and is identical to full history by construction" — rather than the pooled +1.4/+3.2 pp, which is an average over a subset that is zero by construction and therefore dilutes the finding into invisibility.

**One caveat that must travel with it:** the fail-open policy assumes the system *knows* it missed. A real router does not. `context_sufficiency = 1.000` in every row of the main table is a tautology of that design, not a finding, and should be dropped from any results table rather than reported as a metric. The honest k=5 deployment statement is an upper bound conditional on a perfect insufficiency detector — which is itself a concrete, scopeable R1.1 component (spec §7.5's "expand context on low confidence") and arguably the most valuable thing F0.5 has identified as needing to exist.

## 6. The judge-calibration conclusion is wrong — *superseded by §0; acted on and resolved 14 September*

This is the substantive disagreement in this review.

**What happened:** four cross-vendor judges were calibrated against Opus 4.6 on 160 instances. All four cleared κ ≥ 0.6. All four preserved the method ranking (three at Spearman 0.90, Maverick at 1.00, zero ranking violations). All four failed on `mean |Δ| ≤ 0.05`. The bar was not moved — correctly, per the handoff — and Opus was retained, costing $13.04 of a $15.78 phase where the cheapest calibrated candidate would have cost ~$0.91.

**Why the bar was the wrong instrument:** `mean |Δ|` is a *per-instance* absolute disagreement statistic. It is dominated by symmetric random disagreement, which the report correctly diagnoses (≈95% per-item agreement would be needed, a level Opus's own run-to-run agreement was never measured against). But no claim in this study is per-instance. Every reported quantity is a mean over 145 scenarios or a paired bootstrap of two methods scored by the same judge. Symmetric noise averages out over n=145; a constant strictness offset cancels in every method-vs-method difference. **The only judge property that can corrupt a result here is bias that differs across methods** — and that is not what the bar measured.

**Re-scored on the quantity that matters.** Taking the report's own method-means table and computing each candidate's error on the four contrasts the study actually reports (checklist points):

| judge | dag − full (headline) | dag − tree (check 4) | dag − sliding | dag − semantic (check 3) | worst |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opus 4.6 (ref) | −0.084 | +0.042 | +0.153 | −0.090 | — |
| **Llama 4 Maverick** | −0.092 (**−0.008**) | +0.053 (+0.011) | +0.180 (+0.027) | −0.100 (−0.010) | **0.027** |
| DeepSeek V3.2 | −0.088 (−0.004) | +0.080 (+0.038) | +0.177 (+0.024) | −0.086 (+0.004) | 0.038 |
| Mistral Large 3 | −0.119 (−0.035) | +0.029 (−0.013) | +0.148 (−0.005) | −0.064 (+0.026) | 0.035 |
| Kimi K2.5 | −0.115 (−0.031) | +0.076 (+0.034) | +0.204 (+0.051) | −0.094 (−0.004) | 0.051 |

Llama 4 Maverick reproduces F0's headline contrast to **0.8 pp** and no decision-relevant contrast worse than 2.7 pp, with Spearman 1.00 and κ 0.786, at **$0.0007/call**. DeepSeek V3.2 reproduces the headline to 0.4 pp but drifts 3.8 pp on the tree contrast. Both are at or inside the paired sampling error of these subsample means (~0.02–0.03 per contrast at ~32 instances/method), i.e. these errors are not distinguishable from zero — which is the point.

**What I am not saying.** I am not saying lower the bar after seeing the results; that would be exactly the post-hoc rationalisation §12.2 exists to prevent, and the executing session was right to refuse it mid-run. I am saying the bar tested the wrong quantity, that this is visible from the pre-registered design rather than from the outcome, and that the fix is to **specify a correct bar before the next phase and re-run the adoption decision on the calibration data already sitting in `results/scored/calibration_scores.jsonl` at zero marginal cost.**

**Proposed adoption rule for check 3 (to be frozen before any check-3 call):**
1. item κ ≥ 0.6 *(unchanged)*;
2. method ranking preserved, tie band 0.03 *(unchanged)*;
3. **replace `mean |Δ| ≤ 0.05` with: for every pre-registered contrast, |candidate's contrast − Opus's contrast| ≤ 0.02, with a bootstrap CI on that difference computed from the paired calibration instances;**
4. **add a floor: Opus 4.6's own test–retest on a 40-instance re-judge.** No candidate should be held to a standard the reference judge cannot meet, and the report explicitly notes this was never measured. ~$3 and it settles the question permanently.

Expected saving: the judge line drops from ~$13 to ~$1 per phase, which matters as scenario counts rise for benchmark 1.2.

**Two loose ends:** (a) Grok 4.6 remains untested — `AccessDenied` on this Bedrock account in both regions. Four other cross-vendor judges proving adequate makes chasing Bedrock access for it unnecessary; close that thread rather than reopening it. (b) 32 of 731 Opus judge calls (4.4%) ran on a different inference profile/region after the nine-hour quota stall. If the run was ordered by arm, those 32 sit inside one arm and are a confound rather than noise. **Cheap check worth doing: group the stored per-record routes by arm and confirm they are spread, not clustered.**

## 7. Two findings F0.5 produced without meaning to

**(a) F0's headline result replicated, for free, and the replication is tight.** Because `candidate_oracle@15` selects the same turns as `oracle_dag` on 144/145 scenarios, and its answers and verdicts were freshly generated while `oracle_dag`'s were reused from F0, the gap between those two columns is a clean end-to-end re-measurement — new answer sampling, new judge sampling, and a partial region/profile change included. Pooled, it is **+0.4 pp (Sonnet), +0.5 pp (Haiku), +0.7 pp (MiniMax, both arms fresh)**. F0's `oracle_dag` measurement reproduces to within half a checklist point at n=145. That materially de-risks F0 and should be reported as a replication in the paper — it is the kind of thing reviewers ask for and almost nobody has.

**(b) It also yields an empirical noise floor for the by-family tables.** Across the 36 family × model cells where the two arms hold identical context, the gaps have mean +0.005 and **standard deviation 0.039, max 0.075**. So *per-family swings of 4–7 pp anywhere in F0 or F0.5 are the noise floor, not signal.* This is the concrete number the F0 review's "add per-family bootstrap CIs" recommendation was missing, and it should be used to police the by-family narrative: several family-level readings in both phase reports sit inside it.

One anomaly inside that analysis worth a cheap check: `ambiguous_reference` shows +0.041, +0.042, +0.041 across all three response models — identical context, three different models, the same signed offset. That is more consistency than noise usually produces. The likely explanations are (i) coincidence on an n=8 family where one checklist item is worth ~0.04, or (ii) **the two arms serialise the same turn set in a different order** (the candidate pool is ranked by `(sources, cosine, recency)`; oracle_dag is presumably chronological). If (ii), then the "near-replication" framing is not quite right and there is an order effect worth a paragraph of its own. **Diffing the two arms' serialised contexts on a handful of scenarios settles it at zero cost, and should be done before the replication claim in (a) is written down.**

## 8. MiniMax M2.5: hypothesis supported, with one confound

The exploratory arm supports your prior. M2.5 beats Haiku 4.5 on every method — full history 0.878 vs 0.820, oracle DAG 0.901 vs 0.846, candidate oracle 0.908 vs 0.851 — at a comparable or lower per-answer price, and it is the *only* arm whose `candidate_oracle@15 vs full_history` CI excludes zero (+3.0 pp [+0.2, +6.3]). It also does not show the `compound_turn` granularity penalty that both Anthropic models show (second-request: 0.900 restricted vs 0.900 full, against Sonnet's 0.683 vs 0.792).

Three qualifications before promoting it:

- **Verbosity confound.** M2.5 emits 482 output tokens per answer against Haiku's 201. Checklist scoring rewards coverage, and a longer answer mechanically hits more items. Before treating M2.5's margin as a capability result, re-score a subsample with answers length-normalised or truncated — otherwise a reviewer will (correctly) attribute part of a 5–6 pp gap to output length. This same confound may be what suppresses its `compound_turn` penalty: a longer answer covers both halves of a bundled request whether or not the context was correctly scoped.
- **The bias runs in its favour, which strengthens the finding.** The judge is Opus 4.6 — same vendor family as both Anthropic responders, different from MiniMax. If self-preference is present at all, it works *against* M2.5, so its margin is a lower bound. Worth stating explicitly; it is the cleanest evidence in the project so far that the self-preference concern is not driving results.
- **Latency and price are unresolved.** 8.7 s vs 2.8 s is a product-relevant regression for an interactive Context Explorer even if it is irrelevant to a batch benchmark, and the Bedrock rate is billed at the top of the assumed range rather than confirmed. Confirm the rate from the console before any cost claim goes in a paper.

**Recommendation: promote M2.5 to a full gating arm in check 3**, alongside Sonnet and Haiku, once the rate is confirmed and the verbosity check is done. It is cheap, it is the only cross-vendor responder, and it is currently generating the study's clearest signals.

## 9. Reconciling two carried-forward findings

**`ambiguous_reference` hedging — keep the finding, fix the framing.** The F0 addendum elevated this to "restricting context can measurably increase model hedging… a genuine product-risk finding," and the session-state doc carries it forward as something not to explain away. That remains right at the *item* level. But it now needs its counterweight stated in the same breath, because F0.5 replicates the family-level picture across three models including a non-Anthropic one, and it is lopsided in restriction's favour:

| | restricted (cand@15) | full history | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sonnet 4.6 | 0.833 | 0.635 | +19.8 pp |
| Haiku 4.5 | 0.875 | 0.635 | +24.0 pp |
| MiniMax M2.5 | 0.958 | 0.615 | +34.3 pp |

`ambiguous_reference` is the family where restriction wins *most*, not a family where it loses. The hedging behaviour is a real sub-item cost inside a large net gain. Written as-is in a paper, "restricting context increases hedging on ambiguous queries" would mislead. Write it as: *restriction produces a large net gain on referentially ambiguous queries, with a measurable minority of cases where the model hedges rather than committing — a fallback-policy target, not a reason to widen context by default.* The transcript read the F0 review recommended is still worth doing, now with n=3 models to compare.

**`compound_turn` — the granularity penalty survives, and is model-dependent.** Reported as two sub-conditions, as the F0 review asked. Second-request remains a clean loss for restricted context (Sonnet 0.683 vs 0.792 full; Haiku 0.658 vs 0.775) — the original finding, unchanged and now replicated on fresh answers. First-request is inconclusive rather than favourable (Sonnet +10 pp, Haiku 0, MiniMax −5 pp, n=5, well inside the 3.9 pp noise floor from §7b). And M2.5 shows no penalty at all. So the honest statement is: *turn-level granularity failure on bundled requests is real and reproducible on both Anthropic models, absent on M2.5, and the first-request control is underpowered at n=5.* If this is going in the paper as a limitation of turn-level closures — and it should — the family needs more scenarios, not more analysis.

## 10. Recommended next steps, in order

1. **Build benchmark 1.2 before check 3, not after.** 30–60 turn histories concentrated in the join, resume and `long_noisy_side_thread` families. This is now blocking, not optional: check 3 run on benchmark 1.1 inherits the same "no k is both a filter and high-recall" degeneracy, and the efficiency claim is currently measured at ~1,500 tokens where no reader will feel it. Also grow `compound_turn`'s first-request arm past n=5 and `three_way_join` past n=8 while the generator is open.
2. ~~**Re-run the judge adoption decision on the existing calibration data with a corrected bar**~~ — **DONE, same day, per §0.** Standing judge is Llama 4 Maverick at $0.0007/call. Two follow-ups the extension surfaced replace it: **retire the 160-instance calibration sample** and draw any future one as whole scenarios across all methods (§0a), and **write up the free dual-judge replication of F0** that the enlarged run produced (§0b).
3. **Two zero-cost checks on the stored artifacts, before anything from F0.5 is written up:** (a) diff the serialised contexts of `candidate_oracle@15` and `oracle_dag` to confirm turn *order* matches, which determines whether §7a is a replication or an order effect; (b) group the 32 off-route Opus judge calls by arm to confirm they are not clustered.
4. **Rewrite the F0.5 k=5 section around the conditional result** (§5) and delete `context_sufficiency` from the results tables, where it is 1.000 by construction.
5. **Scope the insufficiency detector as a named R1.1 component.** Every favourable k=5 number in this report depends on a system that knows when its pool missed. That component does not exist and nothing so far measures it. It is the natural first piece of router work and the only router work justified before check 3.
6. **Ranking, not recall, is where the candidate generator should improve.** The report's own diagnosis is right: the five heuristics miss only 2 of 491 gold turns, but `(sources, cosine, recency)` ranking promotes multi-source same-branch distractors above gold turns that only similarity finds. Cosine-first ranking is a one-line change to test. Do this only after benchmark 1.2, or it will be tuned against a benchmark where the cap barely binds.
7. **Carry forward unchanged from the F0 review:** the organic-conversation subset (spec §9.6), per-family bootstrap CIs — now with §7b's 3.9 pp noise floor as the sanity check — and the confirmatory/exploratory split in any writeup.

## 11. What I would not do

- **Do not build the router or the Context Explorer UI.** Check 3 is open and F0.5 has raised its stakes, not lowered them. The insufficiency detector (§10.5) is the one exception, and only because every k=5 claim already presupposes it.
- **Do not cite "check 2 passed" without its qualifier** (§4). It is true and it is narrower than it sounds.
- **Do not run check 3 on benchmark 1.1.** It will produce a semantic-retrieval comparison on histories short enough that retrieval and the oracle see nearly the same turns — the single comparison most likely to decide the paper's contribution claim, measured in the regime least able to separate the methods.
- **Do not quote the pooled k=5 quality number (+1.4/+3.2 pp)** as the deployment figure. It averages a real +4/+10 pp effect against a subset that is zero by construction.
- **Do not reopen the Grok 4.6 question.** Inaccessible on this account, and four alternatives are demonstrably adequate under a correct bar.
